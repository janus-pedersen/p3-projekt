\section{Background}
Statistics Denmark (Danmarks Statistik) published a population projection in 2018, which showed that population growth in Denmark primarily reflects the increase in elderly people. The 65-79-year-old demographic is expected to grow by 7\% over the next 10 years, while the 80+ demographic is expected to grow by 58\%. According to the population projection, the number of elderly people over 80 will grow to 2.5 times larger than today by 2057. This means that it is expected that every tenth citizen after 2053 is older than 80\cite{noauthor_nyt_nodate}. The interesseorganisation Ældre sagen, wrote an article in 2023, in which they state that the demographic group of 65+ year-olds in Denmark take up a fifth of the population\cite{noauthor_aeldre_nodate}.

 The Danish patient handbook (Patienthåndbogen) states that of people older than 65, about 33\% fall at least once a year and that 50\% of those people will fall more than once a year\cite{noauthor_fald_nodate}. According to the Danish Health Authority (Sundhedsstyrrelsen), the most common injury for elderly people is falling\cite{noauthor_forebyggelse_nodate}. Furthermore, the Danish Health Authority found that, as elderly people, you are two to three times more likely to fall again if you have fallen once in the span of a year, in case no action is taken to correct the reason (basis?) for the fall. 

\subsection{Limitations}
The limitations of the report are made from, amongst other things, the requirements for the project. For the project, there was a requirement to make an embedded system. There is also a need to consider those who will use the technology. Earlier in the section, there was a population pyramid. However, despite the projection that there will be more elderly people in Denmark, a study by ældresagen states that the population will not necessarily consist of more pensioners. Part of the reasoning behind this statement has to do with the rising retirement age. By the projection made by Aldersagen, this means that there will be 58.000 further people over the age of retirement, whereas in 2080, it will only be 7.000.\cite{noauthor_vi_nodate} All these numbers are compared to the number of people over the retirement age in 2024. 

Another part of the limitations is set by how the users view the use of technology in healthcare. In a study from 2025, the population's attitude towards municipal use of welfare technology for elderly care was investigated. Three requirements for welfare technology were found: 
1) You need to choose yourself. 
This is in regard to a primarily positive attitude towards the use of welfare technology; however, with a stance that the individual should have the possibility to select or deselect welfare technology.
2) Fear of loneliness. 
This refers to a worry about whether or not the municipal use of welfare technology causes loneliness among elderly people, due to the municipal staff's fewer visits. 
3) The responsibility of municipalities in the use of welfare technology. This reflects the general opinion that the elderly person should not have to pay for welfare themselves if the municipality stops offering the welfare to the people in need. This opinion stretches to the attitude that the responsibility of the municipality also includes paying, installing, operations and data security in terms of the use of welfare technology. \cite{noauthor_undersogelse_nodate}

In the article above, a set of limitations to technology came about. What is also important to note is the experiences of older people with technology, thereby their abilities and lack thereof (challenges hereof/involved). A study by Epinion has delved into what experiences and challenges the elderly demographic experiences when using technology. In the study 35\% agree that the challenges they experience with technology are, to some extent, great or very high. Of these, 10\% say the challenges they face are high or very high; this corresponds to approximately half a million people. 

The study also takes a look at the population's view of how digitalisation impacts them. To this, 40\% of people aged 75 or older feel forced to incorporate digital solutions in their everyday lives without wanting to do so. In terms of younger demographics, 29\% of 55-64-year-olds, 26\% of 35-54-year-olds and 28\% of 18-34-year-olds said the same thing. This leads to a need for digital help, guidance and assistance. 71\% of the population experience needing digital help, whereas 41\% of the population feel that they need this guidance occasionally or more often.

Besides the technological challenges mentioned by this study, 86\% of the population agrees or strongly agrees that being able to talk to a human being is also very important in addition to digital self-service solutions.
