\subsection{Interview}
In order to get an idea of whether the prerequisite thoughts match the actual opinions of the targeted audience, an interview was conducted. The interview consisted of questions regarding views on where to spend one's retirement. There were also questions concerning welfare technology, as mentioned in the background section, and how that might look and work. The following interview laid the groundwork for the functionality and design of the wristband and application. 

The interviewee was an 80-year-old retiree who lives at home. To begin with, she was asked if she would prefer to live in a nursing home or to stay living in her own home, to which she answered that she would like to live in her own home. This aligns with the expectations mentioned in the section regarding the Sustainable Development Goals. To gather empirical data on the anticipation of elderly people’s use of technology in their daily lives, the interviewee was asked if they always carry their phone with them and at what distance their phone is from them most of the time. As an answer to these questions, the interviewee responded that they always carry their phone with them and when leaving their phone somewhere, their phone is on average 1 metre distance from them. This helps providing an estimate to the systems limitations in terms of connection. 

To gain data regarding the attitude towards the use of technological aids, the interviewee was asked the following questions: \textit{Would you be interested in utilizing technological aids that can keep an eye on your well-being?},  \textit{If you were to make use of a technological aid that keeps an eye on your well-being, where would it sit on your person?} and \textit{Would you want to wear a wristband that keeps track of whether you’ve been injured? It would not be a smartwatch, it would only detect falls and inactivity”} To which the interviewee responded that they would like to use a technological aid and that a placement for it could be the wrist. They also answered that they wear a watch and that they could be interested in wearing a technological aid in the form of a wristband, which would aid fall detection and inactivity. Thus, further proving the hypothesis regarding elderly people and the use of healthcare technology, as mentioned in the background. 

Contrary to the belief stated in the state of art section regarding a wish for discretion in terms of technological aids, the interviewee stated that she didn’t find discretion relevant, mentioning \textit{that (...) when you’re that age, you've got something that reveals that you’re a bit badly walking or something.}

It has only been possible to do one interview,  therefore the empiric data for the product development is not equivalent to what is required if the product is to go into production and be suitable for the entire target group.
